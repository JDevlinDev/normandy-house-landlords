Between 2021 - 2025 Normandy House reported $2.4 million in "donations." Residents say it was rent.

Normandy House, Inc. runs sober living apartments in Des Plaines and Chicago. Its federal tax returns report $2,463,339 in gifts and contributions from 2021 through 2025, and no rent. A resident's lawsuit says that money is the rent residents pay every week to keep their homes.

Meet the landlords

The players behind Normandy's $2.4M in "donations"

A charity that collects residents' weekly "donations," the company that owns the building, and the man who runs them both. Rounding out the lineup is CJ Frost, the house manager the suit says wielded the "guest" form.

Follow the money

Five years of "donations"

Every dollar Normandy House reported from 2021 through 2025 is listed as a gift, grant, contribution or membership fee. None of it is listed as rent.

  • Residents pay every week

    The resident who filed suit says he pays $230 a week by money order. Normandy's written rules say "Donations are due on Sunday" and allow "No Exceptions or you will be asked to leave." (Complaint ¶¶ 9, 11)

  • A label doesn't change what rent is

    Cook County's tenant ordinance defines rent as "all payments to be made to the landlord under the rental agreement," whatever the landlord calls them. (Cook County Code § 42-803)

  • $366,000 in rent, paid to someone

    The same return reports $366,000 in rent expense for 2025 without saying who received it. It answers "No" when asked whether Normandy did business with a current or former officer or director. (Form 990, Part IV, line 28a)

  • Who owns the building

    Cook County Assessor records list Brown Str. Six Flat LLC as the taxpayer of record for 1471 Brown St, the Normandy apartment building at the center of the lawsuit. Annual reports to the Illinois Secretary of State list Kenneth Ivaska as that LLC's manager and as Normandy's president. (Assessor record) (Complaint ¶¶ 3, 5)

The lawsuit

What the label is for

A resident reported safety problems to the City. Three days later, the complaint alleges, he was told to get out. These are allegations; the court has not ruled on them.

Filed Oct 30, 2026

Sherrick v. Normandy House, Inc., et al.

Circuit Court of Cook County, Second Municipal District · Case No. 20262005977

  • 3 days from the resident's reports to the City to the threat of same-day removal
  • 5 counts under the Cook County tenant ordinance and the Illinois Landlord Retaliation Act
  • Dec 21 first court date, 1:30 p.m., Courtroom 0202, Skokie

The complaint alleges

  • The resident pays Normandy $230 a week to live in an apartment at 1471 Brown St. Normandy calls the payments "donations" and calls him a "guest."
  • On October 26 he reported missing smoke detectors, ungrounded outlets, unpermitted construction, overcrowding and the "guest" form to the City of Des Plaines.
  • On October 29 the house manager entered his apartment without notice, produced the "guest" form, and threatened to remove him that day.
  • He reported the threat to the Des Plaines Police and served a cease and desist the same day. He remains in his apartment.

It asks the court to

  • Declare him a tenant who can be removed only by court order
  • Void the "guest," "renters' rights" and "leave that same day" terms
  • Bar lockouts and entry without notice
  • Award $7,973.32 in statutory damages

Defendants: Normandy House, Inc.; Brown Str. Six Flat LLC; Kenneth Ivaska; Charles Frost.

Know your rights

Living in a Normandy house?

Thinking of moving in? Ask for a copy of the form you'll sign before you pay anything. Already there? These answers come from suburban Cook County's tenant ordinance and Illinois law. They're general information, not legal advice.

  • 01

    I pay a "donation." Am I a tenant?

    Rent is "all payments to be made to the landlord under the rental agreement." The label on the money order doesn't change that.

    Cook County Code § 42-803

  • 02

    I signed a form saying I have no renters' rights.

    A rental agreement can't waive your rights under the ordinance or Illinois law. A landlord who knowingly uses a term like that can owe the tenant damages.

    Cook County Code § 42-804(F), (G)

  • 03

    Can they make me leave today?

    For a rule violation, a landlord must give at least 10 days' written notice and a chance to fix it, then win an eviction case. Even threatening a lockout is illegal.

    Cook County Code §§ 42-809, 42-813

  • 04

    Can staff walk into my room?

    Outside an emergency, a landlord must give two days' written notice before entering, and can't use entry to harass you.

    Cook County Code § 42-808

  • 05

    I reported the house to the City. Now what?

    A landlord can't retaliate for a complaint to a government agency. Action taken within a year of the complaint is presumed to be retaliation.

    Cook County Code § 42-812 · 765 ILCS 721

  • 06

    Isn't sober living a treatment program?

    Illinois law calls recovery homes without a state license "independent living residences that only provide peer support." Ask to see the license.

    20 ILCS 301/55-40(a)
